AI product management tool: the loop, not a tracker

An AI product management tool should close the loop between what you learn and what you ship. Most installed product tools do not: they bolt generative-AI summaries onto the same task tracker and call it done. Maskin is built the other way — raw insight becomes a testable bet, jointly owned by humans and agents, and the loop closes when it ships (see What is an agentic workspace? for the full model).

✓
Key takeaways
  • Incumbent suites bolt generative-AI summaries onto task trackers; the object model never changes.
  • Maskin converges raw insights into testable bets owned jointly by humans and agents.
  • It complements Shape Up’s bet-not-backlog stance, but as a live loop.
  • Open source under Apache 2.0, so the improvement path is auditable and testable.

What product teams actually need

Product teams run on a learning loop: capture signal, decide what matters, do the work, measure whether it worked. AI changes how much of that loop can run automatically — but only if the loop is modelled, not imagined. A team needs:

  • Signal capture — feedback, market moves, churn risk, competitive shifts arriving continuously.
  • Decision — which signals become bets, with a win condition.
  • Execution — the work itself, done by humans and agents together.
  • Validation — did the bet hit? What do we do next?

An AI product management tool that answers “what does the AI do?” with “it writes summaries” is not a loop — it is a tracker with a narrator.

The incumbent reality

Notion, Asana, Jira, Linear, monday, Coda, and Smartsheet ship AI-generated summaries and automation. But their object model stays a task tracker: none turns raw insight into a testable bet owned jointly by humans and agents. The AI is a layer on top; the object of work never changes. That is why the flood of vendor-agnostic content on “closed loop agents” has found no product to settle on — nobody has tied it to running actual work on an actual outcome.

The bet object: insight → bet, jointly owned

Maskin’s unit of work is the bet: a testable hypothesis with a clear win condition, shaped from raw insight and owned by a human and agents together. Agents bring the signal; humans bring taste. The bet is not a ticket under a backlog; it is a decision that gets validated when it ships.

Bet-based planning compatibility

Shape Up’s “bets not backlogs” doctrine is the closest management counterpart. It aligns with Maskin’s loop, but where Shape Up stays a planning discipline, Maskin makes the bet a machine-readable object that agents can create, work on, and validate. See Bet-based product planning for the doctrine’s own story.

Market context: a tiny wedge, fast-growing

Agentic product management is a small, young wedge: roughly sixty native-AI-in-PM companies, about $500M raised, one unicorn — but funding is up sharply year-over-year. The incumbent response to the trend — Atlassian (Rovo + Teamwork Graph), Notion (Custom Agents), and Linear (Linear Agent) — all moved toward agent orchestration, externally validating the shared human-plus-AI workspace thesis.

How Maskin closes the loop

  • Triggers bring work in from Slack, Intercom, PostHog, HubSpot, Reddit, X, LinkedIn, and competitor changelogs.
  • Marketplace loops wire the agents: customer feedback, market signals, churn & expansion, lost deals → roadmap, competitive intelligence.
  • Validation is built in: when a bet ships, outcomes are measured and the requester is notified.
