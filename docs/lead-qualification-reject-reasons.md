# What 207 rejected prospects taught our lead qualifier
**Of 207 outbound prospects our lead qualifier rejected, 84 (about 40 percent) were rejected for one of two reasons: the company was an agency, consulting or services firm (49), or it was too small (35). Both are facts about the company, so you can check them before you pay to enrich a single contact.**
This is a count from our own CRM, not an industry benchmark. Below: what we counted, what it can and cannot tell you, and what to copy if you are building [lead qualification](/marketplace/lead-qualification/) from scratch.
> ✓
> **Key takeaways**
> 207 rejects of real prospect companies, one main reason each, read by hand.
> Agency or services firm (49) plus too small (35) is 84 of 207, about 40 percent.
> Five reasons cover 157 of the 207, about 76 percent: agency, too small, too big (26), no verifiable company or role (25) and duplicate (22).
> These are prospects we found for outbound. They are not inbound form fills.
> Category edges can shift by a few records. The totals do not.

## What stands out
**Two company-level reasons lead.** Agency and too small together are 84 rejects. What the firm sells and how many people it employs are usually on its website.
**Size rejects come from both ends.** Too small (35) and too big (26) together are 61 rejects, almost 30 percent.
**Some rejects are record hygiene, not fit.** Duplicates (22) and records with no verifiable email (15) say more about the list than about the market.
**One sweep can be lopsided.** On 22 Oct, a sweep of Danish agency engagers produced 29 rejects: 15 too small, 10 agency, 2 wrong lane and 2 with no verifiable company. That is 25 of 29 in the two top reasons. It is one sweep in one market, so read it as an example, not a rate.

## How we counted
- **Source.** CRM contacts at status rejected, read on 26 Oct 2026: 306 records, all unique, stamped between 11 Sep and 26 Oct.
- **Why 207, not 306.** 99 of the 306 were never qualification decisions: 67 monitoring records parked at rejected to keep them out of the funnel, and 32 investors, admin and network contacts, media, no-gos and strays. We left them out.
- **Outbound, not inbound.** All 207 are prospects we sourced for [outbound outreach](/marketplace/outbound/). None is a form fill or a demo request, so do not read this as a view on inbound leads.
- **A few sweeps, not a steady flow.** Of the 306 records, 96 were stamped on 14 Sep and 29 on 22 Oct.
- **One main reason each.** Taken from the sales strategy note, then the rejection reason, then the verdict in the record body. If the text cites an agency, consulting or services exclusion, agency wins. Otherwise the first reason named. 160 of the 306 name a second reason, so one label is a simplification.
- **Counted by reading, not by script.** The totals are firm. Edges between categories such as agency, too small and no verifiable company can move by a few records with a different tie-break.
- **Aggregates only.** These are real people. No person or company is named.

## What the data does not tell you
It does not give a reject rate, because this count does not include how many prospects were sourced in total. It does not say whether a reject was the right call, or what happened to prospects who passed. And it is a rejection list against our ICP. A team that sells to agencies would read the top row as its target list.

## What a team can copy
Check the two company-level reasons before enrichment. In order:
1. Does the company sell services to clients (agency, consulting, services)? If your ICP excludes them, stop here.
2. Is the company under your minimum headcount, or a solo practice? If so, stop here.
Both can usually be answered from the company name, website and headcount, before you spend on contact-level enrichment.
Write your own exclusions down as a list of reasons, stamp one on every reject, and count them after each sweep. That is the method behind this article. The case for moving it onto a loop is on the [lead qualification on a loop](/marketplace/lead-qualification/) page.
