Marketplace / The content calendar

Loop · Content

Run your content calendar in Maskin so your team keeps the final say while Maskin keeps dates, drafts, checks and links in order.

The job

This content calendar template brings posts, videos and pages into one place, with an owner, date, channel and links for each idea. Each post waits for an editor check and its account owner’s go. The work closes when the post is live, its link is saved, and the weekly numbers and next-cycle changes are reviewed.

Replaces: A shared spreadsheet nobody updates · a scheduling tool that only holds post text · an agency retainer for planning · asking a founder to remember what to post

Stays human: Your team supplies founder stories and opinions, sets the voice, chooses which ideas to kill, gives the go on every post, and has the final say on anything in its name.

How the loop runs

Each piece moves through these 12 steps, with a person choosing the idea and approving anything that goes out in their name.

  1. Founders share stories, Maskin gathers customer questions and search demand, and one person picks an idea.
  2. Maskin puts one calendar row per piece with its date, channel, account, owner and links to related pieces.
  3. For each web page, the team assigns a URL, one keyword and a parent page before drafting.
  4. Maskin prepares a run sheet with the screen, steps, three questions and what not to show, then the founder records.
  5. Maskin writes wording from sources in the founder’s voice.
  6. Maskin makes an image or carousel and checks it alongside the text.
  7. Maskin prepares a long video cut, short cuts, captions and a low-resolution preview.
  8. The editor check reads the text and image together and gives a verdict.
  9. The account owner gives the go on each post or in one weekly batch. A post is ready only when the editor check and the owner’s go are both given.
  10. After the owner’s go, the post goes out: on some accounts it posts on its own, while on others the owner pastes it, and Maskin saves the link on the post and sets the status to live.
  11. Maskin puts together the weekly brief showing reach, visits and conversion steps.
  12. The team reads the retro each cycle and approves proposed changes shown as old text and new text.

Trust surface

  • Human gates: The editor check comes first, then the account owner gives the go. Nothing goes out in a person’s name without that owner’s go.
  • Your data stays yours: The calendar lives in the workspace. The template file stays in your browser until you export it, so you keep your own copy.
  • Fails safely: If a step cannot finish, such as when the owner has not given the go, the post waits on the calendar and shows as waiting. It is never published by default.

What this loop is built with

Loop

A closed cycle: something fires it (a signal, a schedule, a click), agents do the work, a human gates the taste-sensitive parts, and it ends on a concrete outcome, not on done for now. Every listing on the Marketplace is a loop.

Maskin loops carry typed outcomes and verdicts that persist across tools, not a scheduled run inside one product.

Agent

A named role a model performs: an SEO analyst, a discovery synthesizer, an outbound writer. Not an AI teammate: a role with a job description, tools it can use, and gates it has to pass before its work leaves the workspace.

MCP

The wire an agent uses to reach the tools your team already uses: the CRM, the docs, the inbox, the analytics. If a human on your team can log in and click, an MCP-native agent can be given the same access under the same gates.

Skill

A packaged capability you attach to an agent: draft a follow-up in our voice, summarize a customer call against our discovery template. Skills are the reusable pieces of judgment your team is already building without realising it.

Inside a loop listing, agents / MCPs / skills appear as “built with” components of the loop, never as top-level marketplace sections of their own.

FAQ

What should a content calendar include?

Keep the date, channel, account and owner with each piece, and link related posts, videos or pages together. A useful calendar also shows each piece’s status, planned posts against a weekly goal for each account, and what still needs review. This makes it clear who owns the next step.

How do approvals work in this content calendar?

Every post has two checks: an editor check of the text and image together, and the account owner’s go. A post is ready only when both are given. The owner can approve one post at a time or give a go to a weekly batch. If the go is missing, the post waits on the calendar.

When is a spreadsheet enough for a content calendar?

A plain spreadsheet is enough when your team can keep dates, owners, channels and approval status up to date in one place, and everyone can see what is waiting. If posts, videos and pages need linked records, approval checks and saved live links, this calendar template brings those details together.
