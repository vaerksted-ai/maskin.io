How the loop runs

Two steps are gates, and nothing skips them.

  1. Idea. Bring a real question or story from your audience into the calendar.
  2. Plan. Maskin plans the month around what your team can really approve.
  3. Draft. Maskin drafts each post to the plan.
  4. Picture. Add an image or carousel that fits the post.
  5. Check (gate). Check each draft for fit, accuracy, and claims before it can move on.
  6. Owner’s go (gate). The owner marks Go; only then can the post go out.
  7. Publish and read the result. After the go, the post goes out and its link is saved on the calendar. Then you read reach and visits per channel, and the cycle starts again at step 1.

Trust surface

  • Owner’s Go: A post without the owner’s Go does not go out.
  • Your material: Maskin works from your ideas and calendar and writes back only statuses and links.
  • Empty slots: If there is no good idea for a slot, the slot stays empty.

What this loop is built with

Loop

A closed cycle: something fires it (a signal, a schedule, a click), agents do the work, a human gates the taste-sensitive parts, and it ends on a concrete outcome, not on done for now. Every listing on the Marketplace is a loop.

Maskin loops carry typed outcomes and verdicts that persist across tools, not a scheduled run inside one product.

Agent

A named role a model performs: an SEO analyst, a discovery synthesizer, an outbound writer. Not an AI teammate: a role with a job description, tools it can use, and gates it has to pass before its work leaves the workspace.

MCP

The wire an agent uses to reach the tools your team already uses: the CRM, the docs, the inbox, the analytics. If a human on your team can log in and click, an MCP-native agent can be given the same access under the same gates.

Skill

A packaged capability you attach to an agent: draft a follow-up in our voice, summarize a customer call against our discovery template. Skills are the reusable pieces of judgment your team is already building without realising it.

Inside a loop listing, agents / MCPs / skills appear as “built with” components of the loop, never as top-level marketplace sections of their own.

FAQ

What should a content calendar include?

A content calendar should show what is planned, where it will appear, who owns it, and whether it is approved. Add the publish date, topic, channel, and a clear status for each post. Set a weekly goal per account, and choose a date to read reach and visits per channel.

How do approvals work in this content calendar?

Each post has an Editor mark and a Go mark. The editor checks the draft, and the owner gives the Go before it goes out. Keep the two marks clear so nobody mistakes a draft for an approved post. You can see both marks beside the post.

When is a spreadsheet enough for a content calendar?

A spreadsheet is enough when one person can keep dates, drafts, and approvals clear without extra reminders. If a small team loses track of what is approved or what worked, a calendar with month, week, list, and board views can make the status easier to scan. Export a copy if you want to keep changes.
