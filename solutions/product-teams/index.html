Solution · Product teams

The operating system every high-performing product team should have.

Your PM week goes into retyping the same context — into Notion for the strategy doc, into Jira or Linear for the epic, into the customer-call summary, into the pricing update. The context never sits in one place; every decision starts by re-deriving it.

The tool where the LLM you’d love to help lives is a fifth tab, and it has none of that context. Every decision gets re-derived because the last answer lived in someone’s DMs. New hires read four wikis to catch up on one quarter. Discovery is a rolling backlog of PM interviews and Jira tickets that nobody reads back after they close.

Maskin is the operating system underneath your product team: one workspace where the wiki, the customer signals, the shaped bets, and the delivery handoff live together — ship each bet your way, either as an epic in your existing Jira or Linear tracker or through Maskin’s own delivery agents.

Why these three

Three layers, in dependent order. Nothing in the second layer runs without the first; nothing in the third is worth casting without the second.

  1. Business-context foundation. A single wiki every agent reads before it answers — replaces the six-doc scavenger hunt across Notion, Confluence, and someone’s Google Doc.
  2. Continuous insight capture. Purpose-built loops that turn support tickets, customer calls, LinkedIn signal, and interview data into tagged, clustered insights — replaces the rolling backlog of PM interviews plus tickets that nobody reads back.
  3. Shaped decisions with owners. Time-boxed bets with Won/Lost criteria written up front and a named owner, drafted from the highest-fit insight cluster — replaces PRDs and backlog grooming that never end and rarely close cleanly.

The three steps a product team walks through in Maskin

1. Knowledge Wiki — the business context every agent shares

Replaces

Spreadsheets, Notion, Confluence — a scatter of docs a human writes and forgets to update, and a new hire reads four of them to catch up on one quarter.

What Maskin adds

Agents keep the wiki current. Every decision, every closed bet, every ratified position writes back automatically; every agent reads the wiki before it answers, so no context is stale by the time it’s used.

What the workspace does

In the first minutes, a Researcher agent drafts a business-context sweep against your company URL: what you do, who you serve, who your competitors are, where the market moves. Seven business-context domains cover humans, org, product, customers, competitors, market, and goals & bets. You confirm each item with one tap — looks right / correct this / wrong entirely — instead of typing from scratch. From that point on, every agent reads the wiki before it answers. As decisions get made, closed bets, ratified positions, and customer-truth insights land as pages your team and your agents share.

How the Maskin Team runs it

Every fresh Maskin workspace, including the Maskin Team’s own, gets seeded by the same onboarding checklist and reads its own wiki the same way. The Maskin Team’s rolling opportunity map, its strategic positions, its customer-truth insights all live as wiki pages the team and its agents pull from — no more re-briefing an LLM on who you are, no more decisions re-derived because the last answer lived in someone’s DMs.

2. Product Discovery — install the loops that produce insights

Replaces

Jira or Linear as the discovery backlog, plus PM interviews the PM runs, transcribes, tags, and mostly forgets. Signal gets captured; signal doesn’t get clustered; opportunities die in a ticket queue.

What Maskin adds

Insights auto-cluster into JTBDs and opportunities the moment they land. Every insight arrives already tagged, sitting next to the roadmap it should shape. Clustering, scoring, and the human-gate all live inside each loop — you turn on the loops, the loops do the discovery work, you spend your week on the taste calls instead of the retyping.

What the workspace does

Product Discovery isn’t a single agent you talk to — it’s the set of insight-generating loops you turn on, each one purpose-built to capture a different kind of signal and cluster it into opportunities you can act on. Two loops turn on by default for a PM/CPO team; the rest are opt-in per ICP.

  • Signal Intake loop. Support tickets, sales-call transcripts, review platforms, canned NPS. The loop listens on the sources you connect, tags every inbound signal against the wiki’s product taxonomy, clusters recurring themes into JTBDs, and posts a ranked shortlist for the human gate each week.
  • Customer Feedback loop. Direct customer interviews, feature-request forms, in-app feedback capture. The loop drafts the interview against your discovery template, transcribes the call, extracts the load-bearing quotes, and files them under the JTBDs they belong to — no more losing the interview to a Notion page nobody reads back.
  • Optional insight loops per ICP. LinkedIn signal capture (the buyer’s public thinking), competitive intelligence (release notes and pricing pages), customer-intelligence loops for research shops. Which ones you turn on depends on where the freshest signal lives for your team; the setup checklist walks you through the picks.
How the Maskin Team runs it

The Maskin Team runs Signal Intake plus Customer Feedback on its own workspace, plus a LinkedIn signal capture loop for the founder-led selling motion — different sources than a PM/CPO team would use, same pattern. The product-discovery loop catches market-vocabulary corrections, search-intent misses, and category-positioning shifts as they happen. A recent product-onboarding rework got its scope from three separate loop-landed clusters surfacing the same friction; nobody argued at the shortlist stage because the evidence was already underneath the cluster.

3. Shaping Bets — cast shaped bets from greenlit opportunities

Replaces

PRDs that get half-read and backlog grooming that never ends. Tickets carry into the next sprint, then the next; nobody writes back what actually shipped or whether it worked.

What Maskin adds

Shaped bets carry evidence and ownership on their face. Every bet is a time-boxed outcome with a named owner, Won/Lost criteria written up front, the underlying insight cluster attached, and a written result when it lands — not a ticket that migrates forever.

What the workspace does

Shaped bets replace backlogs. Once a human greenlights a cluster from step 2, a shaping agent drafts the bet’s shape — scope, finish line, owner suggestion, timeline — for you to fold, tighten, and cast. The workspace tracks each bet from shaping through active through live-or-lost, and writes the result back into the wiki so the next quarter’s shaping is informed by what actually shipped.

How the Maskin Team runs it

Every commitment the Maskin Team runs is a bet, with Won/Lost criteria written up front, so nobody’s arguing at the end about whether it worked. When a bet closes, the result is written back into the wiki. Next quarter’s shaping reads what actually shipped, not what people remember shipping. Shipping itself — the delivery motion — happens along either of the two implementation paths in the section below; the bet in Maskin stays the source of truth for the shape.

What this workspace is built with

Named the same on every Maskin page so the shape is portable. This block is explicitly the glossary — the one place on the page where Maskin’s vocabulary earns its keep. Everything above this block reads without it.

Loop

A closed cycle: something fires it (a signal, a schedule, a click), agents do the work, a human gates the taste-sensitive parts, and it ends on a concrete outcome — not on done for now. Every listing on the Marketplace is a loop. Maskin loops carry typed outcomes and verdicts that persist across tools, not recurring workflow execution inside one product.

Agent

A named role a model performs — a Researcher, a Signal Analyst, a discovery synthesizer. Not an AI teammate: a role with a job description, tools it can use, and gates it has to pass before its work leaves the workspace.

MCP

The wire an agent uses to reach the tools your team already uses — the CRM, the docs, the inbox, the analytics. If a human on your team can log in and click, an MCP-native agent can be given the same access under the same gates.

Skill

A packaged capability you attach to an agent — summarize a customer call against our discovery template, draft a follow-up in our voice. Skills are the reusable pieces of judgment your team is already building without realising it.

Ship bets your way — pick per bet, not per team

Path A

Keep your existing tracker (Jira, Linear)

Your engineers live in Jira or Linear. Maskin doesn’t ask them to move. Bets shaped in Maskin export as epics or issues into your existing tracker through a connector your team doesn’t build. The bet in Maskin stays the source of truth for the shape — scope, Won/Lost criteria, owner, timeline. The tracker mirrors what needs to ship for the engineers who live there all day. Your team keeps the delivery motion it knows; you upgrade the discovery, shaping, and signal side of the workflow without a rip-and-replace.

Best for: established product teams with a working delivery motion, ICs who don’t want to learn a second tracker, engineering leadership that treats tracker migrations as high-cost.

Path B

Run delivery inside Maskin (Product Delivery loop)

For teams building AI-native workflows or looking to compress delivery cycle time, the Product Delivery loop runs the bet end-to-end. AI agents — a Planner, a Developer, a Code Reviewer, a Release Verifier — execute the shaped bet against your repo. Humans review at every taste-sensitive step. The loop closes when the change is live and the success signal is confirmed. The wiki gets the result.

Best for: teams shipping AI features, product teams with tight engineering constraints who want to expand what a small team can commit to, teams already comfortable reviewing agent-drafted output.

The choice isn’t team-wide — it’s per commitment. A team running Jira for eight quarters can push most bets into Jira and run one Product Delivery loop for the AI-heavy work. Nothing about starting with Path A commits you to it forever, and nothing about running one Product Delivery loop forces the rest of the roadmap into it. Both paths ship the result back into the same wiki; the shape of what got shipped is legible either way.

Common objections

“But our team is small.”

Good. The workspace is calibrated for small teams. There’s no formal role architecture to fill in — a Researcher, a Signal Analyst, a Copywriter, a Delivery agent play the roles a two-person product team doesn’t have headcount for. Small teams tend to see the biggest lift, because the alternative is one PM retyping the same context four times a week and losing every third insight to their DMs.

“But we don’t have engineering time for a new tool integration.”

The workspace doesn’t need one. Connectors to Jira, Linear, Notion, HubSpot, Intercom, GitHub, Slack, Google Docs, Zendesk, and the rest are already built. Sign in, pick the sources, the first agent has data by the end of the day. If a tool your team relies on isn’t wired yet, name it — building a new connector is a one-week ticket, not a quarter.

“But we already tried an AI PM tool before.”

Fair. Most AI-flavoured PM tools of the last cycle were a chatbot grafted onto an existing tracker: talk-to-your-roadmap, summarise-my-tickets. Maskin is a different shape. The workspace isn’t chat on top of Jira — it’s the layer where the wiki, the customer signals, and the shaped bets actually live, so the LLM finally has context to reason against. The bet you shape in Maskin is a first-class object, not a prompt on top of one.
