The sales loop workflow

Cold prospects in, approved outreach and routed replies out. Research, personalise, human approval on every send, delivered via the sender lane you already trust — with override rate tracked so oversight stays real, not theatre.

What it does

The sales loop workflow takes a single outbound target — a person plus an account, in one signal — and runs the arc from research to approved send to routed reply. It reads the signal, pulls context on the person and the account (title, recent activity, company shape) from the sources you already pay for, and drafts a first message the person would actually recognise as written for them. It parks the draft in your workspace with a needs-approval status and pings the human owner. On approval, it sends via the sender lane you configured — Unipile for LinkedIn, Postmark or Resend for email — and starts watching for a reply. When one comes back, it is routed into the workspace as a fresh signal that can trigger the next step (reply drafted for approval, meeting proposed, conversation closed), so the loop keeps closing without you re-loading spreadsheets.

Who it's for

Sales leads, revenue operators, and founders running outbound in-house. The workflow is built for the person whose bottleneck is the research-and-personalise step, who wants to send more relevant first messages than a mass-blast tool allows, and who is unwilling to let an agent send anything without a human approval gate. If you already have a sender lane you trust (Unipile, Postmark, Resend) and a CRM you already write to (HubSpot, Attio, Salesforce), this workflow sits above them.

What closes the loop

The bet closes when a routed reply lands in the workspace and either advances to a meeting, is deferred to a follow-up, or is written off — not when the message was sent. Sends without replies are steps, not outcomes. Override rate — the share of drafts a human edits or rejects before send — is tracked as the honest oversight metric. Zero override means rubber-stamping; above twenty percent means the draft model is broken; the middle band means the human is actually reading. That number is a first-class signal, not a footnote.

How it works (below the fold)

The workflow is composed from four primitives that ship in Maskin. If you're evaluating it as a system to run on your own instance, this is the shape of what's underneath.

  • Loop

    AI SDR loop

    Steps: signal → research → draft → human approval → send → reply routing → close. Human sits on the approval gate.

  • Agent

    SDR Researcher

    Pulls person + account context from the sources you already pay for; hands off to the drafter with a research brief.

  • Agent

    SDR Drafter

    Writes the first message against your voice examples; parks it for approval with the reasoning trail visible.

  • Agent

    Reply Router

    Watches inbound replies, classifies them, and turns them into fresh signals so the loop advances (or gets closed).

  • MCP

    Unipile + Postmark + CRM

    Sender lane on LinkedIn / email; write-back to the CRM you already use. All scoped, all approval-gated.

  • Metric

    Override rate

    First-class metric on the workflow. Zero = rubber-stamp; >20% = broken drafts; middle band = real oversight.
