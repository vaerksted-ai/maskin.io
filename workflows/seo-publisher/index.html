The SEO publisher workflow

One shaped SEO bet in, one indexed, snippet-eligible article out. Research, draft, human approval, publish to your static site, sitemap and llms.txt registration, ranking tracked — the loop closes on a ranked page, not on a written draft.

What it does

The SEO publisher workflow takes a single shaped bet — "own the SERP for <keyword> with a page that ranks by <date>" — and runs the whole arc without you babysitting the middle. It reads the current SERP and picks the anti-frame that isn't yet claimed. It drafts an outline against that frame, then a full article, and posts it to your workspace as a content object with a proposed status. A human reviewer skims, either approves or comments, and the workflow only continues when it's approved. On approval, the workflow builds the HTML page against your static-site conventions, bakes in JSON-LD, canonical, Open Graph, and per-page og-image, commits it to your site repo, and updates sitemap.xml and llms.txt so crawlers and agents both know it exists. Then it tracks ranking against the target keyword weekly and reports back when the bet is won — or when it needs a rewrite.

Who it's for

Product teams that treat SEO as a growth channel, not a content-marketing side quest. The workflow is built for the person who already has a static site, a shortlist of keywords they want to own, and a bar for what "good" looks like — and wants the mechanical parts (research, drafting, publishing, sitemap-hygiene, tracking) run by agents while they keep taste on the calls that need judgment (which bets, which drafts, which rewrites). If you're a growth lead or a founder-operator running SEO in-house, this is the workflow that removes the busywork without removing the taste.

What closes the loop

The bet closes when the page is indexed and hitting its target rank — not when the draft is written and not when the commit lands. Every other event in the middle is a step; the win condition is on the bet itself. When the target is hit, the bet flips to won and the outcome is logged against the workflow. If the target isn't hit within the appetite, the workflow proposes a rewrite bet from what it learned in the SERP data. Nothing gets stuck in "draft complete" without a decision.

How it works (below the fold)

The workflow is composed from four primitives that ship in Maskin. If you're evaluating it as a system to run on your own instance, this is the shape of what's underneath.

  • Loop

    SEO content loop

    Steps: research → draft → human approval → publish → track. Each step is an agent; the human sits on the approval gate.

  • Agent

    SEO Researcher

    Pulls SERP data, competitor pages, and keyword volume via the Ubersuggest MCP; picks the anti-frame that isn't claimed.

  • Agent

    SEO Writer

    Drafts the article as a content object with primary_keyword and content_type, ready for human review before publish.

  • Agent

    SEO Publisher

    Builds the HTML page, JSON-LD, canonical, per-page og-image, and commits to your static-site repo via the GitHub MCP.

  • MCP

    Ubersuggest + GitHub

    Read-only for keyword and audit data; commit access to your site repo for the publish step. Both scoped, both reversible.

  • Trigger

    Weekly rank check

    Re-audits published pages against their target rank; fires a fix task when a page slips or when a bet's appetite has burned.
