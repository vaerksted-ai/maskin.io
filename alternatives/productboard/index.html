Productboard alternative: what to use instead depends on the job you are missing

The best Productboard alternative depends on which job you are missing. For feedback intake, look at Canny. For a roadmap inside Jira, Jira Product Discovery. For portfolio planning, Aha or ProductPlan. If the gap is that customer signal, the decision and the shipped result sit in separate tools, Maskin keeps all three in one connected record: signals are insights, the bet replaces the PRD, and loops report the result back.

Key takeaways
  • Pick by the job you are missing, not by a ranked list. Feedback intake, roadmap, discovery and delivery are different jobs, and most "Productboard alternative" lists flatten them into one ranking.
  • Productboard is strong at feedback intake and roadmapping. Its own pages describe a customer feedback portal, prioritized backlogs, collaborative roadmaps, 25+ integrations and an AI layer called Spark that drafts specs.
  • Productboard does not publish prices. Its pricing page shows two plans, Business and Enterprise, priced by the number of "makers", with a request-a-demo step instead of a number.
  • Maskin is a different shape, not a drop-in clone. It has no customer portal, no voting and no roadmap views. It is an open-source (Apache 2.0) workspace where signal, decision and result are one linked record. It is early-stage, with a hosted version.
  • You can keep Jira or Linear. Maskin covers the discovery and shaping side, and hands delivery to the tracker you already run.

What Productboard is good at

Productboard describes itself as "the leading product management platform built for scale" (Productboard product page). Read as a list of jobs, that page covers four:

  • Collecting feedback. "Centralize feedback and surface opportunities & insights with AI," plus a customer feedback portal and segmented trend views.
  • Prioritizing. Organized, prioritized backlogs across a product portfolio.
  • Roadmapping. "Collaborative, contextual roadmaps" for keeping stakeholders aligned, and release planning.
  • Drafting. Spark, which it calls an "agentic product system", and AI-assisted specs that draw on your product and customer context.

It also lists 25+ native integrations and open APIs. If your team's bottleneck is getting feedback from many sources into one prioritized place and showing a roadmap to stakeholders, that is a real, mature answer, and nothing on this page should talk you out of it.

Why teams look elsewhere

We kept this to what is visible on Productboard's own pricing page, because review-site complaints are easy to misquote.

  • No published price. The page names two plans, Business and Enterprise, and sends you to "Request demo" or "Contact sales" rather than showing a figure. Comparing against a tool with a public price takes a sales call first.
  • Pricing scales with makers. Productboard defines makers as the product managers and leads who own strategy, and prices by their count. Contributors (engineers, designers, customer success) can submit feedback without being makers: 25 are included on Business and unlimited on Enterprise. If your whole team shapes product decisions, the maker line is the one to check.
  • AI use is metered. The page lists 500 AI credits per maker per month on Business, and 1,500 base credits plus 800 per additional maker on Enterprise.
  • Some governance sits on the higher plan. SAML single sign-on, custom roles and permissions, and a Salesforce integration are listed under Enterprise only.
  • The record ends where delivery starts. This is our reading, not a Productboard statement. Productboard now drafts specs with AI, so the old gap of "no PRD in the tool" has narrowed. What it does not claim to hold is the result: whether the thing you shipped moved the number the bet was meant to move.

How Maskin differs

Maskin does not try to be a better feedback inbox. It changes what the unit of work is.

  1. Customer signals are insights. An interview note, a support ticket or a call summary becomes an insight object that people and agents can both read and write.
  2. The bet replaces the PRD. A bet carries the goal, the evidence (links back to the insights), a timeline, and a written result. The Won and Lost criteria are written before work starts.
  3. Loops run the work and report back. A loop is a closed cycle: something fires it, agents do the work, a person gates the judgment calls, and it ends on a recorded outcome instead of "done for now".
  4. Everything links back. A shipped change connects to the bet, and the bet to the signals behind it. Nothing is re-typed between tools.

Here is the practical difference. In a roadmap tool, a feature request is a row you rank. In Maskin, the same request is an insight you attach to a bet, and six weeks later the bet has a written result next to the evidence that motivated it. When someone asks "did that actually work?", the answer is on the bet, not in a retro nobody wrote down.

Maskin is MCP-native, which means AI agents such as Claude connect to the workspace through the Model Context Protocol and read and write the same objects people do. If you want to see the pieces, the product discovery loop is the input side (signal to shaped bet; it is on a waitlist at the time of writing) and the product delivery loop is the output side (shaped bet to verified code, with a person approving the merge). The method underneath is described in bet-based product planning, and the product teams page walks through the full path for a team that wants to try it.

Open source and the free angle

Maskin is open source under Apache 2.0. You can self-host it for free, so the software costs nothing, and you pay for your own infrastructure and model usage. There is a hosted version if you would rather not run it. See self-hosted AI workspace for what self-hosting involves.

For the search "Productboard free alternative", this is the honest answer: the free options are either open source (Maskin) or free tiers of the tools in the table, and we have not verified any competitor's free-tier limits. Check each vendor's current page before you commit.

Productboard vs Jira Product Discovery

Both help you decide what to build. Jira Product Discovery sits inside the Jira you may already pay for and says its job is to "connect discovery to delivery in Jira," so roadmaps "stay in sync." Productboard is a standalone platform, and its own pages put more weight on feedback intake and a customer portal. If your engineering team lives in Jira and your discovery needs are modest, Jira Product Discovery is the lower-friction choice. If feedback volume and stakeholder-facing roadmaps are the problem, Productboard is the heavier tool for it. Maskin does not replace either: it can hold the bet and its evidence while Jira remains the delivery tracker.

Productboard vs Linear

Linear is a delivery tracker that has added intake: its homepage lists Asks, Insights and customer feedback converted into issues. Productboard is a prioritization and roadmap tool that feeds delivery tools. They solve different jobs. If you are choosing one because you are small and ship from Linear every day, start in Linear and add a feedback tool only when intake becomes the bottleneck. Keeping Linear for delivery works with Maskin as well; see the open source Linear alternative page for where Maskin's model differs from issues and cycles.

Productboard vs Notion

Notion gives you timeline views, databases and sprint workflows and connects to GitHub, Jira, Figma and Slack. It will do almost anything, and that is the cost: you build and maintain the feedback-to-roadmap system yourself, and nothing enforces it. If you already run your wiki in Notion, lightweight discovery there can be enough. If you have outgrown that and want typed records and agents that act on them, see the open source Notion alternative. For the delivery-tracker side of the same question, the open source Jira alternative page compares the tickets-and-backlog model with the bet model.

When to stay on Productboard

Stay on Productboard if any of these is true:

  • You need a customer-facing portal with voting. Maskin does not have one.
  • Stakeholders consume roadmap views. Maskin does not have roadmap views.
  • You run a large product org. Productboard describes itself as built for scale, and Maskin is an early-stage project.
  • You depend on its 25+ integrations or a Salesforce connection. Check Maskin's integrations against your stack before you assume parity.
  • Your team is happy and the price is settled. A tool that works is not a problem to solve.

Maskin also makes no claim about importing your Productboard data. Plan on starting a new workspace and carrying over only the signals and decisions that still matter.

A ten-minute test before you switch

Take your last shipped feature and try to answer three questions from the tool alone, with no one's memory:

  1. Which customer signals made you build it?
  2. What did you say would count as success before you started?
  3. Did it work, and where is that written down?

If your current tool answers all three, keep it. If you can answer the first and not the third, the gap is the one Maskin is built for. If you cannot answer the first, fix intake before anything else, and a feedback tool such as Canny or Productboard is the right place to start.

FAQ

What is the best Productboard alternative?

It depends on the job you are missing. Canny for feedback intake and voting, Jira Product Discovery if you run Jira, Aha or ProductPlan for portfolio and roadmap planning, Linear if delivery is the priority, and Maskin if you want signal, decision and shipped result in one connected record. Most lists mix these groups into one ranking, so decide the job first.

Is there a free Productboard alternative?

Yes, with limits. Maskin is free to self-host under Apache 2.0, and you pay for your own infrastructure and model use. Some competitors offer free trials or free tiers: Aha mentions a free 30-day trial and Jira Product Discovery offers a free option, but check current terms, since we did not verify limits.

Is there an open source Productboard alternative?

Maskin is open source under Apache 2.0, but it is not a clone. It has no feedback portal, voting or roadmap views. It replaces the PRD with a bet and keeps signals, the decision and the written result in one record. If you need a portal today, it is not the right match.

How much does Productboard cost?

Productboard does not show a price on its pricing page. It lists a Business and an Enterprise plan, priced by the number of makers, and directs you to request a demo or contact sales. Business includes 25 contributors and Enterprise unlimited. Get a quote from the vendor, because any figure quoted on a third-party page may be out of date.

What are the main Productboard competitors?

The names that come up most are Canny, Jira Product Discovery, Aha, ProductPlan, Linear and Notion, plus Airfocus in related searches. They overlap on prioritization and roadmaps but differ in what they treat as the core job: intake, planning, roadmaps or delivery.

Can Maskin replace Productboard?

For some teams, on the discovery and shaping side. Customer signals become insights, the bet replaces the PRD, and delivery stays in Jira or Linear or runs through Maskin's own loops. It cannot replace the parts of Productboard that face customers or stakeholders, such as the portal and roadmap views.
