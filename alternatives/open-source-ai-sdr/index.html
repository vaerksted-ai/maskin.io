Open source AI SDR: six GitHub repos compared, and which one fits a founder-led sales team

An open source AI SDR is software you can inspect, adapt and run yourself to find prospects or handle parts of outreach. Six repos fit that description today, but none is turnkey. Choose based on the outbound work you need, the stack you already run, the licence you can accept, and who will install, test and maintain it.

This page is for a founder, owner or head of sales at a B2B company who does outbound themselves and has no SDR team. You are deciding whether to keep a closed AI SDR subscription, adopt an open source stack, or stay with your current process.

What you take on when you leave the subscription

A subscription buys more than access to a model. It can take routine operations off your plate, while a repository gives you code and a README, not a staffed service. You still choose the audience, connect accounts, test the workflow, watch failures and decide who may send. A founder, owner or head of sales doing outbound alone should count those hours as part of the cost. You also carry the risk of a bad configuration, a broken integration or an overlooked unsubscribe. Before you switch, write down what happens from finding an account to handling its reply. Mark who owns each step, what needs approval and how a failure is caught. If nobody can own those jobs every week, the lower software bill may be a false saving. Closed subscriptions commonly cover work such as:

  • Warming sending domains and managing inbox reputation.
  • Handling bounced messages.
  • Tuning prompts and templates using reply data from many customers.
  • Syncing with your CRM and routing replies.
  • Managing unsubscribes and suppression lists.

How to pick between the six

Choose eracle/OpenOutreach if you want a self-hosted Python CLI that finds matching people through a licensed data provider, judges fit, explains each match and emails from your mailbox. It asks for your product and target market, not an uploaded list. Choose jay-sahnan/signal if you want a chat-first, single-tenant workspace on your Supabase and Anthropic keys for buying signals, enrichment and email sequences. These are the most recently active repos. Signal's README says AGPL-3.0 with an optional carve-out for tagged files; read LICENSE because GitHub did not recognize it.

Choose ZeroGTM if you already run n8n and want its Maps-to-outreach pipeline. Choose revenue-os if its Personize, Apollo, Trigger.dev and other integrations fit, but note Early Access and possible API changes. Choose ai-sdr-agent only for reply handling alongside another sender. open-sdr is stale and helps with research, not sending. GPL-3.0 and AGPL-3.0 are copyleft; MIT is permissive. If you only run a tool internally, copyleft rarely bites, but ask someone before embedding or reselling.

A 20-account test before you commit

  1. Pick 20 real accounts you would be glad to win.
  2. Run the same 20 through your two shortlisted options.
  3. For each draft, record whether you would send it unchanged, edit it or bin it.
  4. Track setup and fixing time in hours.
  5. Compare cost per approved draft, including your hours.

Good fit, acceptable drafts and manageable setup time point toward a repo. If the subscription produces drafts you trust and saves time you do not have, that points toward a closed service. If neither option makes useful drafts or the workflow is not worth the effort, keep your current process or do nothing for now.

When a closed AI SDR is the better call

Choose a closed AI SDR if nobody on your team can debug a webhook or keep integrations working. It may also be the safer call if your sending domain is your main company domain, where one bad batch could hurt email deliverability, or if CRM sync must work on day one. Closed services can handle operational work that a repo leaves to you, but check exactly what is included before signing up. If your outbound volume is too low to justify setup and weekly maintenance, the subscription may still be wasteful. In that case, do less outbound or keep it manual until you have a clear reason to automate. Do not buy a system just because the demo looks active.

Put a person on every send

Start with a human approving every draft before it goes out. A mistaken batch can damage the reputation of a sending domain, and mistakes are harder to catch once messages leave. Track how many drafts you change or reject, and why. That shows whether the system understands your offer, audience and tone. If you approve every draft unchanged for weeks, you have stopped reviewing (more on this in human in the loop); sample and check the work rather than treating approval as a click. Maskin is an open-source (Apache 2.0), self-hostable workspace that records agent work in one shared place where a person can review it before it goes out; if your outbound is spread over several tools, that review step is the part to design first. FAQ

What is an open source AI SDR?

An open source AI SDR is software whose source code is available for inspection and use under its licence, designed to automate some sales development work. That work may include finding prospects, researching accounts, drafting outreach or handling replies. The six repos here cover different parts of that job, and each still needs setup and ongoing ownership.

Is there a free open source AI SDR?

You can access these repos without buying a closed AI SDR subscription, but that does not make the full workflow free. Hosting, data providers, model or API access, email tools and setup time may have costs. The revenue-os README claims $50 to $200 a month in API costs, but that is its own estimate, not an independent figure.

How much does an open source AI SDR cost to run?

There is no single running cost. It depends on the repo, its required services, your volume and the hours spent installing, configuring and fixing it. The revenue-os README claims $50 to $200 a month in API costs compared with $2,000 to $5,000 for closed AI SDRs. The claim excludes your time and is not independently verified.

Which open source AI SDR should I start with?

Start with the repo that matches your current stack and the work you need. OpenOutreach finds and emails matching people; Signal watches for buying signals and drafts sequences; ZeroGTM suits people already using n8n. revenue-os is Early Access, ai-sdr-agent handles replies only, and open-sdr is stale and does not send.

Can an AI SDR replace a human sales rep?

No, not end to end. These tools can automate parts of prospecting, research, drafting or reply handling, but someone still needs to choose the market, judge the output, handle exceptions and manage relationships. A B2B company doing outbound itself may save time on particular tasks, but should not treat an AI SDR as a complete sales team.

Do I need a human to approve what an AI SDR sends?

Yes, especially when you begin. Approve every send while you learn how the system behaves. Watch how many drafts you change or reject, and check that unsubscribe and suppression handling is covered. A bad batch can hurt a sending domain's deliverability. If you have approved every draft unchanged for weeks, you are no longer meaningfully reviewing them.

Is an open source AI SDR better than a closed one?

Neither is automatically better. An open source repo can suit you if you can install, configure and maintain it, and its workflow and licence fit your business. A closed service can be the better call if you need operational support or CRM sync on day one. Compare the complete workload, including setup and your time, not just the software cost.
