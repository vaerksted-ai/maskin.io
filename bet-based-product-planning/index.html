Bet-based product planning: the anti-backlog doctrine

Bet-based product planning is the anti-backlog doctrine: instead of a growing queue of tickets, the team runs a loop — insight → bet → task → shipped → repeat. Maskin is built on this loop and makes the bet a machine-readable object that humans and agents own together (see What is an agentic workspace? for the full model).

Key takeaways

The anti-backlog doctrine

Shape Up, the influential planning doctrine, argues that backlogs decay: tickets accumulate, become aspirational, and stop being decisions. Its answer is to plan in shaped bets — appetite-bounded chunks of work with a clear outcome — and a fixed cycle instead of a running queue.

The doctrine has a genuine following in product teams. It is also contested: it is criticised as company-specific and rigid, with teams reporting they switch back. That makes bet-based planning a documented niche rather than a settled norm.

The bet as a shared, testable object

Most planning tools treat a "bet" as a label on a ticket. Maskin makes the bet a first-class typed object:

  • A bet is shaped from raw insight — a signal, not a guess.
  • It carries a win condition — the outcome that validates it.
  • It is jointly owned — agents bring the signal and the execution; humans bring taste and the approval.

When a bet ships, the loop does not stop: the outcome is measured, the requester is notified, and the next bet is shaped from what was learned.

Why it pairs with AI agents

The insight-to-bet pipeline is exactly what agents are good at: monitoring sources continuously, synthesising raw signal, drafting the hypothesis. The human's value slides to what Shape Up always needed — taste: which bets deserve the cycle, and which outcomes actually matter.

The evidence: a live niche

Shape Up is a real movement — teams adopt it, write about it, defend it. The criticism is equally real: it can be company-specific and rigid, and teams switch back. Maskin's approach is not shape-up-as-a-service; it is the loop the doctrine argues for, run on typed objects that agents can work.

FAQ

Is Maskin a Shape Up tool?

It implements the loop Shape Up argues for — bets, not backlogs — on a machine-readable object model; the doctrine's specific rituals are yours to keep or not.

Do bets need a win condition?

Yes — a bet without an outcome is a ticket.

What happens after a bet ships?

Validation closes the loop: outcomes are measured, the requester is notified, and follow-up bets are shaped from results.
